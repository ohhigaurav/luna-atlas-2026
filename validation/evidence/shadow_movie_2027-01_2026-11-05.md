- Coverage: 360 frames, every 2 h from 2027-01-01 00:00 to 2027-01-30 22:00 UTC.
- Grid: 320×320 px at 250 m (±40 km).
- Output: `frames.u8` (36.9 MB), `lit.u8` (4.6 MB), `movie.mp4` (1024², 24 fps, 15 s, 2.2 MB).

counts any visible part of the disc, while the display shading uses the centre's incidence angle.

## Post hoc observation, not a gate

The month's best-lit pixel is at 89.45°S, 223.2°E: lit in 360/360 frames, with the next best at
98.9%. It lies about 1.5 km from "point B", 89.44°S, 141.8°W = 218.2°E, on the
Shackleton–de Gerlache ridge. Point B is Bussey et al. 2010, Icarus 208, Kaguya topography,
annual illumination; the coordinates are as tabulated in Wikipedia's "Peak of eternal light"
article. The ridge is also where Gläser et al. and Mazarico et al. place the best-lit sites.

This is one month in southern summer against an annual average, found after looking, with no
criterion set beforehand. Say "the forecast's brightest spot sits on the ridge the literature
names". Do not say "we found the peak of eternal light".

**Same spot as our Site 001.** The brightest pixel is about 150 m (under one pixel) from our own
Site 001 reference point (LOLA Site 001, Connecting Ridge, 89.45°S, 222.69°E). That site was
chosen for its lighting, so this is consistent, not independent. Better wording: "the forecast's
brightest spot this month is Site 001, on the ridge the literature names".
